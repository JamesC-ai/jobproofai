Title clarity

Explain an internal title without rewriting the record

Keep the official employer title, employer, and dates unchanged. When the title is unfamiliar outside the company, add a supported functional description so a reader can understand the work without mistaking it for an unverified promotion.

Source facts

Start from the employer record you can support

  1. Record the official title, employer, dates, and the source that names them, such as a permitted offer letter, HR record, pay statement, or employment-verification response.
  2. List actual duties, recurring work, scope, tools, outcomes, and dates separately from the official title.
  3. Mark each field confirmed, partial, missing, or uncertain. Preserve the original wording and do not make a missing record look confirmed.
  4. Keep an approver, reference, or source note only when you are permitted to retain it and it is necessary for your own review.

Reader clarity

Use a functional description, not a substitute record

A functional description can explain what an opaque internal title meant in practice. For example, place a plainly supported description beside the official title or in a responsibility line. It must not replace the official title on forms that ask for the employer-record title, and it should never imply a different level, reporting authority, profession, or employer.

Verification readiness

Make any difference easy to explain

Keep a short note that connects the official title to supported responsibilities and dates. If an application, background-check form, regulated role, or employer asks for a specific format, follow its actual instructions and disclose the official title where required. Ask a qualified adviser when the consequence of a mismatch matters.

Hard boundary

Do not manufacture a stronger title

Never invent a promotion, seniority, management responsibility, dates, employer, title, or scope. Do not sign back into a former employer's systems, keep credentials, request confidential HR records, or ask former colleagues to disclose protected information. Use only records you are permitted to review.

No hiring prediction

Evidence organization only

JobProofAI does not verify background checks, employer records, legal disclosure, ATS compatibility, interviews, or hiring. It does not contact an employer, submit an application, decide how a verifier will interpret a title, or promise that a title explanation will be accepted.

Keep the working map private

Remove employee IDs, addresses, manager contact details, compensation, credentials, customer data, and confidential metrics. A clear description is not a reason to expose private employment records.
